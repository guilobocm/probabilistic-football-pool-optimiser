# Limitations and Threats to Validity

Any scientific postmortem requires rigorous identification of its flaws. What appeared to be a resounding methodological success exhibits fundamental weaknesses.

The following claims are and will remain expressly **prohibited** in any instance of this project, based on the threats documented below:

- **"Full pipeline validation"**: False. Only the output was verified.
- **"Absence of data leakage (temporal leakage)"**: Impossible to prove without the original inputs certified in Tier A.
- **"Representative evaluation of the 50-35-20 pool"**: False. Only 4 of the 72 decisions were audited; the 68 invisible predictions limit generalisable inferences.
- **"Evaluation of intrinsic probability (Log Loss, Brier)"**: Impossible. The vectors were not retained.
- **"Scientific proof of the AI architecture in Football"**: Invalid generalisation due to being a narrow temporal sample.

Below are the detailed concrete threats.

## 1. Unverified Inputs and the Risk of Leakage

No base input (`odds_input.csv`) with external certification prior to kickoff was saved in the extraction to the cloud. Operationally, we do not know what the exact market odds were upon entry to the model on the 10th of June 2026. 

There is a mathematical chance, even if inadvertent (e.g., stagnant API caches), that market information already contaminated by last-minute premonitory signals was inserted into the dataset. The model underestimated the observed goals, but the absence of inputs and an ablation by component prevents attributing this bias to the odds or to any specific stage of the pipeline.

## 2. Absence of the Complete Probabilistic Distribution

The model outputted discrete picks (`pick_a`, `pick_b`), but the complete latent probabilities ($P(Win)$, $P(Draw)$, $P(Loss)$) underlying each decision were lost to oblivion, as the text-based artifact submitted by the OpenAI API exposed only the prediction. Without this per-match distribution reallocated and saved (only the "most probable prob" was saved), we cannot measure:
- **Log Loss** (Cross-Entropy), the gold standard of stochastic classification;
- **Ranked Probability Score (RPS)**, to capture if the model, when erring, erred closely (ordinal scale);
- **Brier Score 1X2**.

## 3. Collection Vulnerability (Tier B)

The evidence capture was **subsequent** to the tournament, supported by timestamps (metadata) retained on the origin platform (OpenAI). 
The robustness of this validation is, therefore, held hostage by the internal workings of the provider's API. Although the corporate seal provides a "reasonable degree of certainty" against clock fraud (compared to local clocks), the extraction in raw format (HAR network logs) was not preserved initially. A derived and targeted capsule was extracted subsequently from the platform's authenticated response.

## 4. The Sample Size Problem (n=71) and Dependent Events

The 2026 World Cup, although expanded, remains a singular finite sample (N=72, with N=71 tested).
Furthermore, matches are not necessarily independent, as national teams appear repeatedly and share the context of the group, opponents, and tournament dynamics. The per-match bootstrap does not model this structure and may underestimate uncertainty. The true degree of uncertainty of the optimisation (`Uplift CI = [-22, +42]`) is brutal and perfectly capable of crossing the neutral line.

## 5. Selection Bias in the Flips (50-35-20 Pool)

The 4 flips of the 50-35-20 pool evaluated constitute **a case study, not a population statistical test**. They were recovered specifically because their discrepancy with the Classic model made them central subjects of the annotations generated by the AI in the text log. The performance (+15 points of uplift in these four matches) is an anecdotal event that does not statistically authorise us to proclaim the global victory of the reward optimiser under the 50-35-20 rules in a universe of 72 predictions.
